    How I Work

    Engineering principles · what I bring to a team
    01

    Design the system, not the ticket.

    Every feature lands inside an estate — portal, finance, logistics, LMS. I map the data flow and the failure modes across systems before I write a line of Apex.

    02

    Bulkify, then automate.

    Governor limits are a design constraint, not a bug to fix later. Once it's solid, remove the human step entirely — usage-based billing, self-registration, single sign-on.

    03

    Integration boundaries are contracts.

    Each system owns specific records. Syncs are safe to re-run. Failures surface as work items, not silent log lines. Boomi for orchestrated sync, Kafka for events, EDI where partners live.

    04

    If it hurt to debug, build the tool.

    Apex Doctor exists because 30-minute log triage was a tax on the whole team. Good engineering leaves behind tooling and documentation, not just closed tickets.

    Usage-based US billing on Chargent & finance sync

    Tarana Wireless · 2025 → present · Salesforce · Chargent · Boomi · NetSuite · Kafka

    Problem

    US customers pay for what they use, so every month finance had to work out each customer's usage, raise the invoice by hand and collect payment separately — then carry the results into the ERP. Slow, error-prone in the one place errors cost the most, and it got worse with every new customer.

    Approach

    • Usage-based monthly billing on Chargent — each customer's charge is computed from actual usage for the month, not a fixed recurring amount.
    • Customers authorise a payment mandate once (with auto top-up); Chargent collects each month's invoice automatically against it.
    • Invoice generation and charge runs driven by scheduled and batch Apex, designed around governor limits from day one.
    • Boomi → NetSuite ERP integration, in progress: Boomi flows and NetSuite saved searches keep financial and customer records in sync with Salesforce; Kafka carries events that can't wait for a batch.

    Design decisions

    • Chargent over custom payment code. Card data and PCI scope stay with the payment gateway; we own the usage → invoice logic, which is where the business rules actually live.
    • Metering separated from charging. The month's usage is computed and reviewable before the charge run, so a bad reading never becomes a bad charge.
    • Two integration lanes, clear ownership. Boomi for orchestrated, auditable ERP sync; Kafka for low-latency events. NetSuite owns the ledger; Salesforce owns the customer and the billing schedule.

    Experience Cloud onboarding, Case dashboard & SSO

    Tarana Wireless · 2025 · Experience Cloud · LWC · Flows · SAML · LearnUpon

    Problem

    Every new customer was onboarded by hand — account setup, portal access, training-platform credentials — 20–30+ times a day. Slow for customers, expensive for the team, and sign-ups from personal email domains were quietly polluting account data.

    Approach

    • Built the full Experience Cloud site: self-registration, guided onboarding flows, and a self-service portal.
    • Domain verification at sign-up: company-domain emails map to the right Account; anything else lands in a guest-level Dummy Account with an on-screen prompt to switch.
    • SAML SSO from Salesforce into LearnUpon — one click into training, no second set of credentials.
    • Rebuilt the customer Case dashboard from scratch as a Lightning Web Component, retiring the legacy UI.

    Design decisions

    • Salesforce as the identity provider. User lifecycle lives in one place; deactivate once and access is gone everywhere.
    • Guest access instead of rejection. A real prospect on a personal email isn't blocked — but they can't see another customer's data.
    • Clicks first, code where it earns its place. Flows for the guided steps; LWC where the UI had to do more than Flow screens allow.

    EDI integrations across 3PL & partner ERPs

    Tarana Wireless · 2026 · Salesforce · EDI · 3PL / WMS · Customer & distributor ERPs

    Problem

    Orders, shipments and fulfilment updates moved between Salesforce, the 3PL warehouse and customers' own ERPs by email, spreadsheet and re-keying. Every hop was a delay and a chance for a mismatch nobody noticed until a customer did.

    Approach

    • EDI integrations connecting Salesforce with the 3PL / logistics warehouse system and with customer & distributor ERPs.
    • Document mapping between Salesforce objects and each partner's format; acknowledgements and error handling so a failure surfaces instead of silently dropping.
    • Salesforce stays the single view of order and fulfilment status for support and sales.

    Design decisions

    • Each partner interface is a contract. Explicit document types, versioned mappings, and a named owner for every field.
    • Fail loudly. Rejected or unacknowledged documents become work items, not lines in a log.
    • Thin integration layer, rules in Salesforce. Adding a partner is a new mapping, not a new project.
